SHEET METAL THEORY // K-FACTOR
VISUAL REFERENCE: BENDING MECHANICS & FORMULA SHEET
Theory & Fabrication Guide

K-Factor in Sheet Metal

Understanding bending mechanics, neutral axis calculations, and flat pattern generation.

Concept in Simple Language

When you bend a sheet of metal, the outer surface stretches (tension) and the inner surface crushes (compression).

Somewhere in the middle, there is a layer that neither stretches nor compresses. This is the Neutral Axis.

The K-Factor is simply the ratio of this Neutral Axis position to the total sheet thickness. It tells CAD software (like SOLIDWORKS) how much the metal will stretch so it can generate an accurate flat pattern for cutting.

Common Technical Interview Questions

Q1: What is K-Factor and why is it critical?
A: K-Factor is the ratio of the distance from the inside face to the neutral axis (t) divided by the total sheet thickness (T). It is critical because it directly controls the flat pattern dimensions. Using the wrong K-Factor results in incorrect part lengths after bending on the press brake.
Q2: What is the typical range of K-Factor?
A: The K-Factor always ranges between 0 and 0.5. Under normal bending parameters, a standard default value commonly used is 0.33 (steel/aluminum).
Q3: How do tight bend radii affect the K-Factor?
A: A tighter bend radius pushes the neutral axis closer to the inner surface, resulting in a lower K-Factor (e.g. 0.25 to 0.30). A wider bend radius keeps the neutral axis closer to the middle, resulting in a higher K-Factor (e.g. 0.40 to 0.50).
Q4: Given a sheet thickness of 2 mm and K-Factor of 0.33, where is the neutral axis?
A: t = K * T = 0.33 * 2 mm = 0.66 mm from the inner face.
RANGE LIMITS: 0.0 TO 0.5
STANDARD DEFAULT: 0.33

Theory & Fabrication Guide

Bend Radius in Sheet Metal

Understanding minimum bend radius, material limits, and tooling selection.

Concept in Simple Language

Bend Radius (R) is the inside curvature formed when sheet metal is bent. It is always specified by the designer and measured from the inside surface.

Selecting the correct radius is critical: if a bend is too tight (small radius), the material will undergo high stress, risking cracking on the outside or kinking on the inside.

Larger bend radii prevent cracks, reduce springback, and make the part much easier to form on a press brake.

Common Technical Interview Questions

Q1: What is Bend Radius and how is it measured?
A: Bend Radius is the radius of the inside curvature of a bend. It is measured from the inside surface of the bent sheet metal to the center of the bend arc (it is not measured to the neutral axis).
Q2: Why is selecting the correct Bend Radius critical?
A: It directly affects part strength, appearance, and manufacturability. If the radius is too small, the material undergoes extreme tension and cracks. It also controls springback—tighter bends have less springback, but carry higher cracking risk.
Q3: What are the standard recommended minimum bend radii?
A: Minimum bend radius is defined as a factor of thickness (t). Common ranges:
  • Mild Steel (CR): 0.5t - 1.0t
  • Stainless Steel: 1.0t - 2.0t
  • Aluminum: 0.5t - 1.5t
Q4: Given a sheet thickness of 2 mm and a bend factor of 1.0t, what is the inside bend radius?
A: R = 1.0 * t = 1.0 * 2 mm = 2 mm inside bend radius.
MINIMUM RADIUS: 0.5 * THICKNESS (0.5t)
DESIGN BEST PRACTICE: USE LARGEST POSSIBLE RADIUS
